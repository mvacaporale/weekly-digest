# Weekly Digest — Sat Oct 31 – Fri Nov 6, 2026

Democrats take both houses of Congress as the US and Iran trade fire over Hormuz again, Somalia's top court ends the president's term and Paraguay's president falls over a bank collapse. In tech, a fake Claude Code extension shows developer tools are now the attack surface, AI starts repairing other AIs, and practitioners show how agents really get used at work.

## The World

4 things happened this week that you'll still care about in five years.

**1. Democrats won both the House and the Senate, so Trump faces a Congress run by the other party for his last two years.**

*The background you need:* Every two years, halfway through a presidential term, the US holds "midterm" elections for the whole House of Representatives and about a third of the Senate. Republicans have held both chambers since January 2025. That let Trump pass laws and get appointments confirmed with little resistance. Congress controls federal spending, investigations and impeachment, and the Senate confirms judges and cabinet officials.

*What happened:* On Nov 3, US news organizations projected that Democrats will control both the House and the Senate in the 120th Congress, which starts in January 2027. Some of the wins came in places Democrats had not won in decades. Adam Hamilton became the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Kansas since 1932. James Talarico won Texas's first Democratic Senate seat since 1988, and Gina Hinojosa became the first Democratic governor of Texas since 1990. Abdul El-Sayed won in Michigan and will be the first Muslim US senator. Keisha Lance Bottoms won Georgia's governorship, becoming the first Black woman ever elected governor in the US. US tech stocks fell sharply after the result: one outlet reports the Nasdaq dropped more than 5.5% on Thursday.

*Why you'll care later:* This is the biggest change in US governance since 2024. Trump's legislative agenda now effectively stops. Every judge and official he nominates needs a Democratic Senate to confirm them. Congressional investigations, subpoenas and budget fights will set the terms of the 2027–28 period. The war with Iran, the trade wars with Canada and China, and the 2028 election will now be fought against a hostile Congress. Losing Kansas and Texas also suggests the electoral map may be changing, not just swinging back and forth.

*Thread to pull:* What powers does a Congress controlled by the opposition actually have over a president's foreign wars and tariffs, through war-powers resolutions, appropriations and trade law? How have past divided Congresses, such as 2007 under Bush, used them?

**2. The US and Iran are shooting at each other again in the Strait of Hormuz.**

*The background you need:* The US and Iran have been at war since Feb 28. Through October the fighting had wound down into a tense standoff, with diplomacy run through Qatar and a group of Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials. The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow channel between Iran and Oman that roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through. Iran has been trying to claim legal authority over the strait and threatened foreign shipping "from Nov 1." Trump had hinted that strikes could resume after the midterms.

*What happened:* On Nov 2, Iranian surface-to-air missiles fired at American aircraft over the strait. The US responded with airstrikes on Iranian targets along it, and explosions were reported on Iran's Qeshm Island (AP, Al Jazeera). On Nov 3, an unidentified object hit a commercial ship off Musandam, Oman's tip of land on the strait. One Indian sailor was killed and 13 crew were rescued (Reuters, citing India's foreign ministry). The IRGC, Iran's elite military force, also said a tanker hit a mine just south of the strait. The war's economic effects keep spreading: Australia's central bank raised rates to 4.85%, its highest since 2008, citing war-driven energy costs. A week earlier Canada's had done the same.

*Why you'll care later:* This is the inflection point to watch for. The de-escalation that held through October has broken, and the US and Iran are exchanging fire directly over the world's most important oil chokepoint. Until now, attacks on ships were Iranian claims that the US disputed. A crewman killed on a ship, confirmed by India's government, means attacks on commercial shipping are a reality again. Two Western central banks raising rates because of the war in two weeks shows its costs are now reaching ordinary mortgages far from the Gulf.

*Thread to pull:* What happened to oil prices, shipping insurance rates and tanker traffic through Hormuz in the week after Nov 2, 2026? Is the Qatari peace draft still alive?

**3. Somalia's top court ruled that the president's term ran out six months ago, just as foreign troops are leaving.**

*The background you need:* Somalia, on the Horn of Africa, has been fighting the al-Qaeda-linked militant group al-Shabaab for nearly two decades. Its government has survived largely because of outside help: a UN support mission and African Union troops, many of them from Uganda. President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud pushed through constitutional amendments that critics said were designed to extend his time in power. His government has also fought with some of Somalia's regional states.

*What happened:* On Nov 5, Somalia's Supreme Court struck down the constitutional amendments. It ruled that the president's term actually expired on May 15, 2026, and ordered new elections within 90 days (AFP, Somali Guardian). Three days earlier, Uganda began withdrawing its troops from Somalia, planning to pull out completely by the end of 2027 after almost twenty years (AFP). The UN's assistance mission wound down on Oct 31.

*Why you'll care later:* A fragile state's top court has declared its sitting president illegitimate at the moment its foreign security support is leaving. If he defies the ruling, Somalia faces a crisis over who legitimately governs it, just as al-Shabaab loses its main opponents on the ground. If he complies, Somalia gets a rare court-forced transfer of power. Either way, the arrangement that has held Somalia together since the late 2000s, a weak central government protected by foreign troops, is ending.

*Thread to pull:* Who are the main candidates and power brokers if Somalia holds elections in early 2027? Which foreign forces besides Uganda's, such as Ethiopian, Egyptian, Turkish and US, are still on the ground there?

**4. Paraguay's president resigned after a bank collapse wiped out people's savings, and Congress moved to impeach him anyway.**

*The background you need:* Paraguay is a landlocked country of about 7 million people between Brazil, Argentina and Bolivia. It is a major soy and beef exporter and one of South America's more stable economies. Santiago Peña, a conservative former finance minister and central banker, took office in 2023. ueno is a fast-growing digital bank with links to his government.

*What happened:* ueno collapsed, freezing the savings of millions of Paraguayans. On Nov 5 Peña announced his resignation, and minutes later the Chamber of Deputies voted unanimously to start an impeachment trial against him (AP, Reuters).

*Why you'll care later:* A government fell, and it fell because of a banking failure, not a coup or a protest movement. That makes this a test case for a risk that is new to the 2020s: a lightly regulated digital bank that grows big enough to bring down a government when it fails. Watch how far the crisis spreads through Paraguay's banking system and whether neighbors in the Mercosur trade bloc have to step in.

*Thread to pull:* How did ueno bank grow so quickly in Paraguay, what were its ties to the Peña government, and how did regulators miss its collapse?

## AI & Tech

*The X timeline was unavailable this week, so everything here comes from newsletters.*

### The toolchain is the attack surface

**Malware disguised as a fake Claude Code add-on reached thousands of developers' machines.** Cursor warned about 5,000 users that a VS Code extension posing as Claude Code was malicious. AlphaSignal reports it planted SSH backdoors, which give an attacker remote access to the machine. The more coding agents developers install, the more an impostor plugin is worth to an attacker. On the defense side, AlphaSignal also reports (headline only) that an OpenAI model found and patched 948 Linux kernel exploits for $1.2M. *(AlphaSignal)*

**Anthropic will pay experienced technical people $350K–$500K a year to switch into AI safety research, with no ML background required.** The residency runs 6 weeks to 6 months full-time and teaches machine learning on the job. Residents get a mentor, compute and a scoped project in interpretability, AI control, oversight or red-teaming. Short placements can be remote, and some residents may go on to full-time roles. Applications close **November 22**. *(AlphaSignal)*

### AI working on AI, and on science

**One AI fixed another AI by editing its internals directly, with no retraining.** MIT CSAIL had GPT-6 Astra inspect a small Qwen model that kept getting stuck in reasoning loops. Astra changed just 0.016% of its weights, and the small model's math score rose from 7.9% to 48.1%. The repaired model is on Hugging Face. Separately, a study tested whether Claude Code can improve itself by reviewing its past mistakes. AlphaSignal's reading: generating plausible fixes is easy, but checking whether they actually work is still the hard part. *(AlphaSignal)*

**AI agents are starting to run research end to end, though so far these are headline claims.** AlphaSignal reports three separate results without much detail. Google's autonomous research agent beat human scientists by 25% across 86 tasks. A Tokyo lab staffed by AI agents ran a full quantum-physics experiment and reports finding a new law. In a test world with made-up physics, GPT-6 Astra was the only one of 12 models to discover the hidden rules; the other 11 scored zero. *(AlphaSignal)*

### Where's the money?

**Outside the tech industry, AI's measurable financial payoff so far is zero, according to Apollo's chief economist.** Torsten Slok [argues](https://apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector/) that business intelligence is mostly judgment, and judgment depends on context. In a separate book-length essay, [*The Commanding Heights*](https://commanding-heights.com/) (free online), Will Manidis and Joe Roberts compare AI labs to locomotive builders, who didn't get the railroad fortunes. Their bet is that the money goes to whoever controls distribution and payments, and that those payments will increasingly run on a stablecoin dollar. *(Readwise Wisereads)*

## Operating Advice

### How agents actually get used

**Figma's product managers share one Git repo of 31 AI "skills" that sets a minimum standard for routine PM work.** Ezra Mechaber's PM OS is just an AGENTS.md file, the skills and a few hooks, kept in sync across 60–70 PMs. A "what's up" skill that he runs about six times a day summarizes Slack, so he archives roughly 10 channels daily. Agents handle bug bashes from a scenario board and catch edge cases humans skip. AutoXP runs growth experiments with only one human check before launch ([writeup](https://www.chatprd.ai/how-i-ai/inside-figma-s-pm-os-31-ai-skills-better-bug-bashes-and-an-automated-growth-loop)). *(How I AI / Lenny's Newsletter)*

**In a head-to-head test of personal AI agents, no single one won; the best setup was different agents for different parts of life.** Claire Vo ran Grok Bot, Dot, Meta's Muse and Claude Duet through nine real tasks. Grok Bot scored 93% overall and won for work, and Muse won for personal tasks and speed. Duet was the slowest and asked for permission four times, against once for each of the others, so it felt like something to babysit rather than trust. Her other finding: differences in connectors (Slack, GitHub) explained a lot of the gap ([writeup](https://www.chatprd.ai/how-i-ai/grok-vs-dot-vs-muse-vs-claude-duet-nine-tasks-four-agents-no-universal-winner)). *(How I AI / Lenny's Newsletter)*

**The next wave of AI apps may rely on small single-purpose models alongside the big chatbot.** Mistral's Timestral forecasts numbers such as sales or energy use without any per-dataset training. Its largest version has just 209M parameters, and it leads three public forecasting benchmarks. Similar models exist for spreadsheet-style tables (TabPFN), linked databases (Kumo Relational) and fixed-choice decisions. Ben Dickson's blueprint is to let the reasoning model plan and explain, hand predictions to the specialists, and keep rules in plain code. The main saving is development effort, not always speed or cost. *(AlphaSignal)*

## Quick hits

- Claude's plans got new names: Max 5x is now **Max** ($100/mo) and Max 20x is now **Ultra** ($200/mo). Verified Ultra accounts lose the 5-hour usage cap, but weekly limits stay. *(AlphaSignal)*
- Claude Code v2.1.316 has a smaller model write background subagents' status updates from short notes instead of rereading the whole transcript. That's about 6% more prompts before you hit your limit; run `claude update` to get it. *(AlphaSignal)*
- Claude API's Message Threads (public beta) keeps the conversation on the server, so an 800 KB request shrinks to 1.8 KB. The first token arrives about 35% faster on very long contexts, but threads expire after 5 minutes, so keep your own history too. *(AlphaSignal)*
- Vercel Labs' open-source agent-desktop (`npm install -g agent-desktop`) lets agents drive your desktop through the operating system's map of buttons and windows rather than screenshots, and can run several agents in parallel on one machine. *(AlphaSignal)*
- OpenAI quadrupled its bug bounty to as much as $172,000 per critical find. Pro subscribers now also get API credits that match their plan price each month ($200 plan, $200 credits), and the credits don't roll over. *(AlphaSignal)*
- Deep Principle's Hydrogen-1.0 is an open, MIT-licensed 753B model trained on real lab records to plan experiments, and it edges GPT-6 Astra on a research-science benchmark (38.3% vs 33.3%). *(AlphaSignal)*
- Wispr Flow's growth engine is 50 creators producing 500–600 paid user-made ads a month, each paid a cut of ad spend. A head of content hired at 19 built it, and revenue grew 30× in a year. *(Lenny's Newsletter)*
